# Token-launch research: funding the parts explorer with a stock-paired token on Robinhood Chain

Research date: 2026-09-25/26. Researcher: Claude (desk research only; ~30 web searches, ~17 page fetches). This is a fact survey with sources, not legal advice. Anything marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed from a primary source.

Scope: a US (Idaho) individual wants to fund an open-source automotive parts explorer by launching a "meme-utility" token on Robinhood Chain, LP-paired against a tokenized stock (Ford, AutoZone/O'Reilly, or a basket), distributing to users/stakers/contributors, with a transfer tax that deepens the pool and a low initial float. Question asked: launch via Long (app.long.xyz) or another Robinhood Chain launchpad?

---

## 1. Summary

1. **Robinhood Chain is live** (mainnet 2026-07-01, Arbitrum Orbit L2, chain ID 4663, ETH gas, Uniswap v2/v3/v4/UniswapX deployed 2026-07-02). The chain itself is permissionless; US wallets can bridge, swap, LP and deploy contracts.
2. **Robinhood Stock Tokens are securities and are not offered to US persons.** They are "tokenised debt securities" issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Limited, giving economic exposure only, sold in the EU as MiFID II derivatives via Robinhood Europe UAB. Robinhood's own docs: they "may not be offered, sold, or delivered, directly or indirectly, in the United States or to, or for the account or benefit of, U.S. persons." The restriction is legal/app-layer, not (as far as found) enforced in the ERC-20 contract. **UNVERIFIED** whether the token contracts carry any transfer blocklist.
3. Therefore the core idea, a US person seeding and operating a pool of Stock Token + own token, means a US person acquiring, holding and continuously "delivering" an unregistered security to counterparties. No source found that blesses this; Robinhood's docs say the opposite. This is the single biggest fact in this document.
4. **Long (app.long.xyz)** is a permissionless launchpad on Robinhood Chain (launched 2026-07-14) where every token is paired against a Robinhood Stock Token (NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, TSLA, MU, SPCX). No platform token, co-founder "Nate", ~20% of the chain's tokenized-stock TVL, Uniswap v4 hook based, "Community Mode" fee vaults, 24h ticker reservation, LongX leveraged wrapper. Its docs (docs.long.xyz) returned 403; fee schedule, LP-lock policy, vesting and tax-token support are **UNVERIFIED**. Ford (F), AZO and ORLY were not in any published pair list.
5. Other live launchers: **Pons** (v2 bonding curve into locked Uniswap v4 pool; pairs incl. ETH, stables and stock tokens; Uniswap Labs bought PONS in Sept 2026), **Pools.trade** (Uniswap Labs' own launcher, fixed 1B supply, permanently locked v4 liquidity, 0.25% fee), **Flap**, **Noxa** (paused July 11 for bot spam), plus hood.fun, Trensh, Bankr, MintPlus. **Team Finance** and **UNCX** lockers/vesting are live on the chain. None of the launchpads documents support for fee-on-transfer tokens; all the major ones use fixed-supply tokens with locked LP and take fees at the pool, not on transfer.
6. **Mechanics:** classic fee-on-transfer (tax) tokens work only on Uniswap v2 via the `SupportingFeeOnTransferTokens` router functions; v3 is explicitly unsupported (funds can get stuck); v4 core does not natively support them but v4 **hooks** can charge a swap fee and route it to buyback/LP, which is how every Robinhood Chain launchpad already does "tax to liquidity". Tax tokens are a documented sniper/honeypot red flag ("raise tax to 99% after launch"). Low-float/high-FDV is a widely documented red flag with insider-unlock dynamics.
7. **US legal status (Sept 2026):** the Feb 2025 SEC staff memecoin statement covers coins with "no use or functionality" whose funds are "not pooled ... for developing the coin" and explicitly excludes products labelled memecoins to evade registration. A token sold or distributed *to fund development* is the opposite fact pattern. The March 17 2026 SEC/CFTC interpretation says tokens become investment contracts when the issuer promises "essential managerial efforts"; airdrops are outside Howey only when recipients give no "money, goods, services, or other consideration" (so contributor rewards for work are consideration). The Aug 18 2026 proposed **Regulation Crypto Assets** (Rule 200: $5M over 4 years, one-time; Rules 300-307: $20M/$75M per 12 months; Rule 400 safe harbor via Form TR) would be a compliant path but is a *proposal* in a 60-day comment period. **CLARITY Act cloture failed 49-50 on 2026-09-15**; market-structure law unlikely before 2027.
8. Idaho: the Dept. of Finance treats virtual-currency exchangers (fiat in, crypto out) as money transmitters; no-action letters exist for selling one's own inventory. Airdropping your own token to contributors is not obviously transmission, but this was not confirmed for this fact pattern.
9. Precedents that survived (Gitcoin, Optimism RetroPGF, Protocol Guild, Drips, Juicebox/Revnets, DIMO, Hivemapper) share a pattern: retroactive or usage-metered rewards, multi-year vesting, immutable or locked treasuries, and a real payer of fees. Memecoin-funded launches that failed (LIBRA, M3M3/Meteora, MELANIA) share insider float, thin locked LP, and are now in US class actions.
10. **Working conclusion (not legal advice):** the Long/stock-paired route is structurally unavailable to a compliant US person because the pair asset is itself a US-restricted security; a Base/Arbitrum memecoin does not solve the Howey problem for a token that funds development; the low-risk, precedent-backed path for an open-source data project is points/retro-grants first (no token), Drips/Juicebox-style donations, and, if real capital is needed, Reg CF now or Reg Crypto Assets Rule 200 if adopted. Ten lawyer questions are listed in section 9.

## 5. Mechanics

### 5.1 Fee-on-transfer (tax) tokens vs Uniswap versions
- **v2:** works only through the `...SupportingFeeOnTransferTokens` router functions, which measure received balance after transfer. ([OpenZeppelin forum](https://forum.openzeppelin.com/t/attention-uniswap-v3-wont-support-anymore-token-with-fees/10221))
- **v3:** "does not support rebasing or fee-on-transfer tokens so using these tokens with it will result funds getting stuck"; Uniswap "will not be making a router that supports fee-on-transfer tokens." ([Uniswap docs: Token Integration Issues](https://docs.uniswap.org/concepts/protocol/integration-issues), [Uniswap/interface #1840](https://github.com/Uniswap/interface/issues/1840), [v3-core #565](https://github.com/Uniswap/v3-core/issues/565))
- **v4:** core PoolManager does not natively support FoT; periphery delta-settlement can tolerate unknown amounts, but auditors flag FoT accounting as a hook hazard. The idiomatic v4 approach is a **hook that charges a swap fee** (dynamic fee, `afterSwap` skim) and routes it to buyback/LP. Note a hook fee only hits swaps through that pool, not wallet-to-wallet transfers, so it is not a "transfer tax" in the v2 sense. Documented hook risks: "liquidity theft via hook fee", hidden sell allowlists (honeypots), one-time-liquidity locks. ([Cyfrin v4 vs v3](https://www.cyfrin.io/blog/uniswap-v4-vs-v3-architectural-changes-and-technical-innovations-with-code-examples), [Trail of Bits, Building secure v4 hooks, 2026-07-30](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/07/30/building-secure-uniswap-v4-hooks/), [Composable Security](https://composable-security.com/blog/uniswap-v4-liquidity-theft-via-hook-fee/), [Uniswap hooklist PRs](https://github.com/Uniswap/hooklist/pull/7334), [Uniswap custom accounting](https://docs.uniswap.org/contracts/v4/guides/custom-accounting))
- **Crypto Briefing (2026-09-03):** v4 hooks on Robinhood Chain are mostly built by independents (Uniliquid, Hookify): volatility-based dynamic fees, anti-sandwich caps, off-hours pricing for stock tokens. ([source](https://cryptobriefing.com/robinhood-chain-uniswap-v4-hooks-tokenized-stocks/))

### 5.2 Auto-LP tax tokens: failure modes
- Classic "tax to liquidity" (SafeMoon lineage) sells half the accrued tax into the pool and pairs the rest; the sell itself moves price and is front-runnable; the contract owner usually retains `setTax`/`excludeFromFee`, which is the "raise tax to 99% after buyers are in" honeypot. Sniper tooling explicitly flags transfer taxes, pausable flags and hidden mint as exit triggers. ([MEXC sniper review](https://www.mexc.com/news/1012768), [Banana Gun](https://blog.bananagun.io/blog/solana-sniper-bots-how-first-block-token-sniping-actually-works), [soltokencreator anti-MEV](https://www.soltokencreator.io/blog/prevent-mev-sniping-solana-token-launch))
- Launch-block sniping: bots buy in the same block as pool creation; anti-rug bots front-run the developer's LP removal at 80-85% success. Pons mitigates with a 2-block initial-buy/wallet cap; Long with 24h ticker reservation; Pools.trade with a 4-hour bid auction.
- "Locked liquidity does not mean safe": mint authority, team float and hook admin keys remain. ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/why-locked-liquidity-does-not-mean-a-token-is-safe/))

### 5.3 Low float / high FDV
- Definition and why buyers discount it: limited circulating supply is easy to move; the price is set by a thin float while insiders hold a scheduled overhang; unlocks then sell into demand that did not grow. Heuristics quoted: <20% circulating, cliff within 3 months, >5% of float unlocking per month, FDV/float ratio >5x. ([Binance Research, May 2024](https://public.bnbstatic.com/static/files/research/low-float-and-high-fdv-how-did-we-get-here.pdf), [Cobie, "private capture, phantom pricing"](https://cobie.substack.com/p/new-launches-part-1-private-capture), [Tokenomist](https://tokenomist.ai/learn/glossary-term/low-float-high-fdv))
- Regulatory angle: the LIBRA, M3M3 and MELANIA complaints all centre on insiders holding a large share of supply before public trading and then selling into thin pools; that is the fact pattern plaintiffs and prosecutors use for "manipulative scheme". A deliberately low float held by the founder is, in those filings, an aggravating fact rather than a neutral design choice. ([DL News 2025 memecoin review](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/how-scandals-and-lawsuits-shaped-memecoins-in-2025/), [Decrypt on Meteora suit](https://decrypt.co/315860/solana-dex-meteora-sued-alleged-pump-dump-meme-coin-launch), [Inc. on MELANIA suit](https://www.inc.com/chris-morris/melania-trump-memecoin-sparks-lawsuit-over-alleged-scam/91254954))

### 5.4 LP locking and vesting
- Team Finance and UNCX both live on Robinhood Chain with v3/v4 position lockers and token vesting. Pools.trade and Pons v2 lock LP at the protocol level, which is stronger than a third-party lock. ([Team Finance](https://www.team.finance/), [UNCX](https://docs.uncx.network/guides/for-projects/liquidity-lockers-v3))

### 5.5 Airdrop / points / contributor distribution tooling
- **Merkle claim contracts:** Uniswap's MerkleDistributor lineage; Sablier Airdrops supports up to 1M recipients per campaign with instant or vested ("Airstream") claims; Sushi's vesting Merkle distributor. ([Sablier airdrops repo](https://github.com/sablier-labs/airdrops), [Sablier Airstreams](https://blog.sablier.com/introducing-airstreams/), [Sushi](https://github.com/sushi-labs/vesting-merkle-distributor))
- **Vesting/streaming:** Sablier, Hedgey, Superfluid, Llama; Splits (used by Protocol Guild). ([Logos vesting ecosystem survey](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/appendix/token-vesting-ecosystem.md), [Splits](https://splits.org/blog/protocol-guild/))
- **Points-first:** off-chain ledger of contributions (PRs, part-number submissions, verified fitments) with a later, optional conversion. Nothing on-chain until there is something worth distributing; this is also how the March 2026 SEC interpretation's "no consideration" airdrop carve-out is *hardest* to reach, because contributors *do* give consideration (see 6.3).

## 6. US legal reality (facts and sources; not advice)

### 6.1 Howey exposure for a token that funds development
- The March 17 2026 SEC/CFTC interpretation: a crypto asset is sold in an investment contract when the issuer makes "representations or promises to undertake essential managerial efforts which would induce purchasers to reasonably expect profits." It can cease to be one after milestones are completed or "publicly abandoned". Non-security categories: digital commodities (value from "programmatic functioning" of a functional system), digital collectibles (NFTs/memecoins with "limited or no functionality"), digital tools, payment stablecoins. Tokenized securities are securities "regardless of whether ... onchain or offchain." ([SEC press release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-30-sec-clarifies-application-federal-securities-laws-crypto-assets), [Sullivan & Cromwell memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/March/SEC-Clarifies-Application-Securities-Laws-Crypto-Assets), [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/sec-clarifies-status-of-crypto-assets-under-federal-securities-laws-signals-potential-exemptive-and-safe-harbor-framework))
- A "meme-utility" token whose stated purpose is to fund and reward development of a product, sold to the public with a low float and a founder treasury, is the "essential managerial efforts" fact pattern, not the "collectible" one.

### 6.2 The Feb 27 2025 memecoin staff statement
- Covers coins with "limited or no use or functionality", where "funds are not pooled together to be deployed by promoters ... for developing the coin", and promoter effort is just "hyping the meme coin." Explicitly excludes products "labeled 'meme coins' in an effort to evade the application of the federal securities laws." Staff statement only: "has no legal force or effect." Commissioner Crenshaw dissented. ([SEC staff statement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/staff-statement-meme-coins), [Crenshaw response](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/crenshaw-response-staff-statement-meme-coins-022725), [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250313-the-state-of-meme-coin-regulation-sec-staffs-statement-and-other-considerations), [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250305-sec-s-corporation-finance-staff-says-the-offer))
- Read against the plan: "fund development" and "distribute to contributors" are the two things the statement says memecoins do *not* do.

### 6.3 Selling vs airdropping vs retroactive grants
- **Airdrop, no consideration:** March 2026 interpretation says distributing a *non-security* asset to recipients who provide no "money, goods, services, or other consideration" fails Howey's first prong. Two limits: (a) the asset must already be a non-security; (b) contributors rewarded *for work* have given services, i.e. consideration. ([Buchanan Ingersoll](https://www.bipc.com/sec-clarifies-the-application-of-the-securities-laws-to-crypto-assets), [a16z on airdrops](https://a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/airdrops-safe-legal-frequent/), [Hunton on the Tomahawk airdrop enforcement case](https://www.hunton.com/blockchain-legal-resource/sec-brings-enforcement-case-involving-airdrop-securities))
- **Reg Crypto Assets proposal (2026-08-18)** would explicitly accommodate "distributions made as rewards or incentives in connection with the use, operation, governance, or security of a crypto network ... including certain airdrops" under the $5M Rule 200 startup exemption. It is a proposal in a 60-day comment period, not law. ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/8/sec-proposes-regulation-crypto-assets-creating-tailored-crypto-offering-exemptions-and-investment-contract-safe-harbor), [SEC proposed rule PDF](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/33-11434.pdf), [Atkins remarks 2026-03-17](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-remarks-regulation-crypto-assets-031726))
- **Retroactive grants** (Optimism RetroPGF pattern) pay for already-delivered work from a treasury the payer controls; there is no purchase and no forward promise. This is the structure in the surviving precedents (section 7).

### 6.5 Pairing with a tokenized equity
Facts found; no source addresses the exact scenario, so this is the highest-uncertainty section:
- The pair asset is a security (tokenised debt security / MiFID derivative) that "may not be offered, sold, or delivered ... to, or for the account or benefit of, U.S. persons" ([Robinhood docs](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/stock-tokens/)). A US person seeding the pool must first acquire it (no compliant route: "no buying stock tokens in Robinhood Wallet, and no compliant secondary route" per [TrustSwap](https://trustswap.com/robinhood/us-availability)), then the pool continuously sells it to buyers of the memecoin.
- Peirce (July 2025): tokenized securities are securities; distributors "must consider" registration/exemption obligations. ([Robinhood 10-K citation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000178387926000023/hood-20251231.htm))
- A token whose price is mechanically linked to Ford through the pool looks like a product "derived from" a security; whether that makes the memecoin itself a security-based swap or a derivative is an open CFTC/SEC question I could not find addressed for stock-paired memecoins. **UNVERIFIED / no source.**
- The Farmmi episode shows a pool on this chain moving a listed stock's price; counsel with market-manipulation expertise flagged the gap. ([Armstrong & Bradylyons](https://www.armstrongbradylyons.com/library/farmmi-memecoin-crypto-market-manipulation))
- Robinhood's own 10-Q (Q2 2026) lists "significant regulatory, litigation, contractual, operational, and reputational risks" from Stock Tokens and third-party DEX access in Wallet. ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001783879/000178387926000114/hood-20260630.htm))

### 6.6 Idaho
- Idaho Dept. of Finance: "Virtual Currency Exchangers" that accept fiat for delivery of virtual currency to a third party need an Idaho Money Transmitter licence; numerous no-action letters exempt sellers of their own inventory and crypto-only (no fiat) venues. Idaho Digital Assets Act classifies digital assets as intangible personal property (signed March 28; year reported as 2025 in secondary source, **UNVERIFIED**). ([Idaho DoF money transmitters](https://www.finance.idaho.gov/securities-bureau/money-transmitters/), [moneytransmitterlaw.com Idaho page](https://moneytransmitterlaw.com/cryptocurrency-state-laws/idaho/), [Bloomberg Law 50-state](https://pro.bloomberglaw.com/insights/technology/cryptocurrency-laws-and-regulations-by-state/), [Manatt on federal prosecution under state MT laws](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/client-alert/federal-prosecution-cautions-crypto-industry-on-state-money-transmitter-laws))
- Idaho also has state securities law (Uniform Securities Act, same bureau); a token sale to Idaho residents is a state-law question as well as federal. Not researched further.

### 6.7 Compliant USD-raising alternatives
- **Reg CF:** up to $5M per 12 months through a registered funding portal/broker; new C&DIs Feb 2026. ([SEC Reg CF guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/regulation-crowdfunding-guidance-issuers), [crowdfundingattorney.com](https://crowdfundingattorney.com/2026/03/17/sec-issues-new-reg-cf-guidance/))
- **Reg A+ Tier 2:** up to $75M/12 months, SEC-qualified offering statement; costly for a hobby-scale project.
- **Reg D 506(c):** unlimited, accredited investors only with verification; general solicitation allowed.
- **Reg Crypto Assets Rule 200 (proposed):** $5M over 4 years, one-time, includes reward/airdrop distributions, principles-based disclosure, no resale holding period. Crowdfunding practitioners are already comparing it to Reg CF/Reg A in comments. ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/09/309579-securities-crowdfunding-og-submits-comments-on-regulation-crypto-assets-compares-to-jobs-act-exemptions-reg-cf-reg-a/))

## 9. Recommendation (research view, not legal advice)

**Do not launch a stock-paired token on Robinhood Chain from Idaho.** The facts, not the vibes, decide this: the pair asset is a security whose issuer says it "may not be ... delivered ... to, or for the account or benefit of, U.S. persons"; a US person cannot compliantly obtain it, and an LP position is a standing offer to sell it. That is before the Howey question on your own token, which the memecoin staff statement expressly does not cover when funds are pooled to develop the product. Long itself is fine as a venue for non-US creators; it is the wrong venue for you.

**Do not rebuild the same idea on Base/Arbitrum** to dodge the pair problem. It removes one exposure and leaves the bigger one (a low-float token sold to fund development by an identifiable founder), and memecoin demand does not correlate with wanting a parts database.

**Recommended sequence:**
1. **Points now.** Keep an off-chain contribution ledger (fitment submissions, part-number verifications, PRs, diagram contributions). Publish the scoring rule. Promise nothing about a token. This is the DIMO "baseline issuance" idea without a token and the RetroPGF idea without a treasury.
2. **Donations via Drips/Juicebox/Giveth + a Gitcoin round** for real cash; route a percentage upstream to dependencies (vPIC, OBDb, rockauto-api forks) so the ask is "fund the commons," which is the story that has survived.
3. **If capital is actually needed** (e.g. to license OEM interchange data or run a shop-facing API): Reg CF through a portal, or wait for Reg Crypto Assets Rule 200 to be adopted and use the $5M startup exemption, which explicitly contemplates contributor reward distributions.
4. **Revisit a token only** when there is a demand side that pays fees (shops, apps) so rewards can be marketplace-metered like DIMO, and only under an adopted exemption with vesting and a locked, protocol-held pool (Pools.trade-style), never a founder-controlled tax switch.

**Open questions for a lawyer (securities + Idaho):**
1. Is a US person who provides Stock Token liquidity in a Uniswap pool "delivering" the Stock Token to US or non-US buyers, and does that make them an unregistered dealer/distributor?
2. Does a memecoin whose price is pool-linked to a single tokenized equity become a security-based swap or CFTC-regulated derivative?
3. Does a token that funds an open-source project, with a founder-held treasury and public roadmap, fall inside "digital collectible" or inside "investment contract" under the March 2026 interpretation? What statements would move it?
4. Are contributor reward distributions "consideration" (services) that defeat the airdrop carve-out, and does that change if rewards are retroactive with no prior promise?
5. If Reg Crypto Assets is adopted, does Rule 200 fit; what disclosures (Rule 103) would apply to a solo maintainer; when would Form TR be available?
6. Idaho: does issuing your own token to contributors, or selling it for ETH, trigger money-transmitter licensing or Idaho Uniform Securities Act registration/notice?
7. Does a "fair auction" on Long or a "Crowd Launch" on Pools.trade constitute an offering by the creator, and who is the "issuer"?
8. Tax: are contributor distributions compensation (1099/W-2 style) at receipt value?
9. Does using vPIC/NHTSA/EPA data in a token-rewarded product create any data-licensing issue with a for-profit reward layer?
10. What personal liability attaches to the founder if a locked-LP token collapses (LIBRA/M3M3 theories: manipulation, unjust enrichment, state fraud)?
